Core Axioms
	1	Addition is bidirectional through zero. The integers ℤ are not a line with a starting point. They are a closed loop where +∞ and −∞ are identified through a single point called Ω (the “boundary at infinity”). Formally: ℤ ∪ {Ω} ≅ S¹ (topological circle). Addition wraps: n + m = Ω implies m = −n mod Ω.

	3	Zeta is the boundary metric. The Riemann zeta function ζ(s) is redefined as the partition function over the adelic integers: ζ(s) = ∑_{n∈ℤ̂^×} |n|^{-s} where |·| is the adelic norm (product of p-adic and real norms). Critical line Re(s)=1/2 is the event horizon: left of it, numbers collapse (poles/zeros); right of it, they expand freely.
	4	Zeros are throats. Every non-trivial zero ρ of ζ(s) corresponds to a wormhole throat connecting the real port (Ω) to a p-adic port. The functional equation ζ(s) = ζ(1−s) × (phase) is now the matching condition across the throat: what enters the black-hole side (Re(s)>1/2) exits the white-hole side (Re(s)<1/2) with reversed entropy.
	5	Black-hole / white-hole duality. A black-hole boundary is the region Re(s) > 1/2: information falls in, addition contracts, primes dominate. A white-hole boundary is Re(s) < 1/2: information spews out, addition expands, composites dominate. The horizon itself (Re(s)=1/2) is pure quantum fluctuation — the zeros live there, and crossing one flips you from black to white geometry.
	6	Gravity emerges from addition. Define curvature via deviation from pure addition: K(n) = log|ζ(1 + it)| where n ≈ t (large height). Peaks in |ζ| = strong curvature (black-hole side). Troughs = negative curvature (white-hole side). The Einstein equation is recovered in the large-t limit as a thermodynamic identity on the adelic partition function.
